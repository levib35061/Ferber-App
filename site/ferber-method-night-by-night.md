# Ferber method night by night: what nights 1 to 7 usually look like

By the parent who makes Check In · Updated November 17, 2026

Knowing what's normal on each night makes the hard ones easier to get through. Here's what to expect, night by night, and what to do when a night doesn't go the way you hoped.

**The short version.** Night 1 is often long. Nights 2 and 3 are often *harder*, not easier. That's the most common point to give up, and the most important one to push through. Most families see a clear change by nights 4 to 7. In one survey of 652 parents, crying averaged about 43 minutes on the first night and about 9 minutes a week later.[2](#s2) Every baby is different, and progress is rarely a straight line.

The waits below follow the [standard Ferber chart](https://ferbertimer.com/#chart). Count only while your baby is crying, keep check-ins to a minute or two, and start again at the first wait for a night waking. Shorter waits are fine if they feel right for your family. Staying consistent matters more than the exact numbers.

## Night 1

Waits: 3, then 5, then 10 minutes

**What it's often like:** the longest bedtime so far. Your baby is used to falling asleep a certain way, and that's changing. Many babies cry on and off for 30 to 60 minutes; some much less, some more.

**What helps:** do the same calm bedtime routine you'll use every night, put your baby down drowsy but awake, and leave. When a check-in comes, go in for a minute or two: a pat and a few soft words, without picking them up. Ferber's own description of a check-in is that it's there "to reassure him (and yourself), not necessarily to help him stop crying."[1](#s1)

**Common worry:** "They cried harder after I left." That's normal. It doesn't mean the check-in failed.

## Night 2

Waits: 5, then 10, then 12 minutes

**What it's often like:** sometimes better, often worse. A rise in crying on the second or third night is common. Your baby is testing whether the old way still works. In one small study, crying rose from about 35 to 40 minutes to over 70 minutes over the first two nights before it fell.[3](#s3)

**What helps:** expect it, and decide in daylight that you'll stay with the plan. If two of you are doing this, agree beforehand on who goes in tonight.

## Night 3

Waits: 10, then 12, then 15 minutes

**What it's often like:** for many families, the hardest night or the turning point, and sometimes both. The waits are now long enough that it can feel unbearable to sit through them.

**What helps:** look at the numbers from nights 1 and 2. Writing down crying minutes each night (the [printable chart](https://ferbertimer.com/chart.html) has space for it) makes it easier to see a trend instead of a single bad night. If you need a shorter wait to stay consistent, use one, rather than abandoning the plan.

## Night 4

Waits: 12, then 15, then 17 minutes

**What it's often like:** for many babies, the first clearly easier bedtime. Crying is shorter and some check-ins are never needed because your baby is asleep before the wait runs out.

**What helps:** keep everything the same. An easier night is the method working, not a sign you can loosen the routine.

## Night 5

Waits: 15, then 17, then 20 minutes

**What it's often like:** bedtime crying is often down to a few minutes, and night wakings start to settle faster too.

**What helps:** treat any night waking exactly like bedtime, starting from the first wait for tonight. Most of the remaining progress happens at 2am, not at 7pm. More on [night wakings and night feeds](https://ferbertimer.com/ferber-method-night-wakings-and-feeds.html).

## Night 6

Waits: 17, then 20, then 25 minutes

**What it's often like:** many babies now fuss briefly or not at all. Some parents find the waits have stopped mattering, because there's rarely a check-in.

**What helps:** keep bedtime and wake-up times steady. A consistent morning start helps nights as much as anything you do at bedtime.

## Night 7

Waits: 20, then 25, then 30 minutes

**What it's often like:** for most families who've stayed consistent, a very different bedtime from night 1. In the survey above, crying averaged about 9 minutes after a week.[2](#s2)

**What helps:** from here on, keep using the night 7 waits. There's no night 8 row; the waits stop growing.

## After night 7

Keep the same routine for several more weeks, even when bedtime is easy. Ferber describes most changes happening "within a few days, two weeks at the most."[1](#s1) If you've been consistent for about two weeks and there's no real change, that isn't your failure, and it's a good time to talk to your pediatrician about what to adjust.

Some families see a flare-up a little after the first week. In informal reports from parents, roughly one in five see a harder night somewhere around nights 7 to 9.[4](#s4) Respond the same way you did on night 1, and it usually passes in a night or two.

## When a night goes backwards

- **One bad night after good ones:** common, and usually a blip. Keep going.
- **Teething, a cold or a vaccination day:** comfort your baby. It's fine to pause and pick the plan back up when they're well. Many families repeat the previous night's waits when they restart.
- **Travel or a big change:** expect a night or two of extra crying, then return to the same waits.
- **Check-ins seem to make it worse:** some babies get more upset by check-ins. Fewer, longer-spaced check-ins can work better for them.
- **Still stuck after several nights:** see [Ferber method not working: 10 things to check](https://ferbertimer.com/ferber-method-not-working.html).
- **Something feels wrong:** trust that. If your baby seems sick, in pain or different from usual, go to them, and call your pediatrician if you're worried.
