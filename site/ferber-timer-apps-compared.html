Ferber method timer apps compared

By the parent who makes Check In · Updated November 17, 2026

You don't need an app to do the Ferber method. A clock and a chart work. But at 2am, tired and anxious, a good timer makes it much easier to stay consistent. Here's what to look for and what's available.

The short version. Any timer works if you use it consistently. The things that matter most in an app are: it times the wait from when crying starts, it restarts at the first wait for a night waking, and it alerts you with the phone locked. We make one of the apps below, Check In, so we've stuck to plain facts about the others and suggest you check their App Store pages for the latest details.

Do you need an app?

No. Plenty of families use a kitchen timer and a printed chart. What an app adds is fewer things to remember in the middle of the night: which night it is, which wait you're on, and when to restart.

What a good Ferber timer should do

  • Count crying, not clock time. If your baby settles, the wait should stop.
  • Restart at the first wait for a night waking, using that night's row of the chart.
  • Alert you with the phone locked, quietly, so you're not staring at a screen.
  • Be usable half-asleep: dark screen, big buttons, easy to undo a wrong tap.
  • Let you adjust the waits, because the chart is a guide.
  • Work for two parents if you're sharing the nights.
  • Show progress, so a hard night doesn't feel like the whole story.

How to choose

  • On a budget, or just trying it: a phone timer and a printed chart.
  • One parent, simple needs: any of the small Ferber timers will do the job.
  • Two parents sharing the nights: look for partner sync, so both phones agree on the night and whose turn it is.
  • Already using a baby tracker for feeds and diapers: see if it does what you need before adding another app.
