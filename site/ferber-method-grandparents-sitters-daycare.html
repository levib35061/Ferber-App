The Ferber method with grandparents, sitters and daycare

By the parent who makes Check In · Updated November 19, 2026

Sleep training is hard enough with two parents agreeing. Add a grandparent who can't bear crying, a sitter for date night, or a daycare with its own nap routine, and it can feel like you're starting over every week. You don't have to control everything.

The short version. Nights at home matter most. Babies learn that different people and places do things differently, so daycare naps and the occasional sitter won't undo your progress. If someone else will put your baby down at home, give them a short written plan: the routine, the waits, what a check-in looks like, and when to just go in.

What has to match, and what doesn't

Ferber's core point is consistency: your baby falls asleep the same way at bedtime and after night wakings.1 That's about your nights at home. Many babies nap one way at daycare and another at home without trouble.

  • Should match: bedtime routine and night wakings at home, whoever is doing them.
  • Can differ: daycare naps, a car-seat nap, a rare night at a grandparent's house.

A one-page plan to hand over

Write it down. People find it much easier to follow a plan on paper than to remember what you said at the door. Cover:

  1. The routine: bath, sleep sack, feed, book, song, lights out, in the crib awake.
  2. Tonight's waits: for example 5, then 10, then 12 minutes (the printable chart has every night).
  3. A check-in: a minute or two, a pat and a few calm words, no picking up, then leave.
  4. Night wakings: start again at the first wait.
  5. When to just go in: vomiting, a fever, something stuck, crying that sounds like pain, or anything that worries them.
  6. How to reach you, and that it's fine to call.

Try: if a sitter is only there for the evening, do bedtime yourself before you go out, so they only have to handle a waking.

When a grandparent stays over

A grandparent can be a huge help, especially taking a night so you can sleep. It goes best when it's agreed in daylight:

  • Walk them through why you're doing it, briefly. "We're teaching her to fall asleep on her own; the check-ins let her know we're there."
  • Agree who goes in. Some grandparents find it easier to sleep through it; others want a turn.
  • If they can't follow the plan, that's fine. Keep their nights for when the baby is already sleeping well, and do the hard first week yourselves.

Daycare naps

Daycares usually have their own nap routine for the whole room. Ask how they put babies down, share how you do it at home, and let it be. Many babies nap better at daycare than at home, perhaps because the routine never changes and other babies are sleeping too.

If daycare naps are very short, an earlier bedtime on daycare days can stop your baby getting overtired.

Keeping everyone on the same page

A shared log helps more than you'd think: the next person can see when the baby went down, how many check-ins there were, and when they last woke. Paper on the fridge works.
