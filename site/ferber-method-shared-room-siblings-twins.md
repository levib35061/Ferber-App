# The Ferber method in a shared room, with siblings or twins

By the parent who makes Check In · Updated November 19, 2026

Most Ferber advice assumes a baby alone in a nursery. Plenty of families don't have that: the crib is next to your bed, a big sister sleeps three feet away, or there are two babies. It still works. You just plan a little more.

**The short version.** The waits and check-ins are the same wherever the crib is. What changes is who else hears the crying. For a week or so, many families move the other sleeper (a parent, a sibling, a twin) somewhere else, add white noise, and start on a night when everyone can afford a rough night. Most siblings sleep through more crying than you'd expect.

## When your baby sleeps in your room

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends room sharing (your baby's own crib or bassinet in your room, never your bed) for at least the first 6 months.[2](#s-aap) So if you start Ferber around 6 months, your baby may well be sleeping a few feet from you.

The hard part is that your baby can see you, and you can hear every sound. A few things help:

- **Block the view.** A folding screen, a bookcase or a hung sheet between the crib and your bed means your baby isn't watching you lie there.
- **Go to bed later.** Do bedtime and the first stretch from another room, and come to bed once your baby is asleep.
- **Treat a check-in like any other.** Get up, go to the crib, keep it short and quiet, then go back to bed or step out.

**Try:** if you have a second room you can use for a week (a spare room or a home office, set up to safe sleep standards), moving the crib there for the training week and back afterwards is a common middle ground.

## A big sibling in the same room

Older children often sleep through more than parents fear. Still, for the first few nights it's usually easier to give the big sibling a "sleepover" somewhere else: your room, a mattress in the living room, or a grandparent's house.

- **Tell them what's happening.** "The baby is learning to fall asleep. There might be some crying, and Mom and Dad are checking on them." Children cope better with crying they understand.
- **White noise** by the older child's bed helps either way.
- **Stagger bedtimes.** Put the baby down first, and bring the older child in once the baby is asleep.
- **Move them back** once the baby falls asleep with little or no crying, usually after a week or so.

If the baby wakes the older child in the night, settle the older child first. A calm big sibling matters more than keeping to the exact wait that one time.

## Twins and other multiples

Each baby needs their own safe sleep space.[2](#s-aap) Beyond that, families usually choose one of two ways:

- **Both at once.** Same bedtime routine, both down awake, one parent per baby for check-ins if you can. Babies often sleep through a twin's crying more than you'd expect, especially after the first few nights.
- **One at a time.** Separate the twins for a week (one in your room or another room) and train them apart, then bring them back together. Slower to set up, but each night is calmer.

Keep the same chart for both babies, but run a separate wait for each: if one is asleep and the other is crying, only the crying baby's wait counts.

## Thin walls, apartments and neighbours

The first two or three nights are usually the loudest. A short note or a word with the neighbours ("we're sleep training our baby this week, sorry in advance") tends to buy a lot of goodwill. A rug, closed windows on the crib side, and white noise in the baby's room all help a little.

## Pick the right week

Whatever your setup, start on a week with no travel, no visitors staying over, and no big changes coming. A Friday start gives two nights where nobody has to be at work the next morning.
