Ferber vs cry it out vs the chair method

By the parent who makes Check In · Updated November 17, 2026

People often use "cry it out" for any sleep training, but the methods are quite different. Here's how they compare, and what the research does and doesn't say.

The short version. Ferber isn't cry it out: you go back in for short check-ins at set times, and the waits grow slowly. Cry it out means not going back in at all. The chair method keeps you in the room and moves you out over a week or more. Reviews of the research find that these behavioral methods generally work, and none has been shown to be clearly better than the others.1 Pick the one you can do consistently.

Is Ferber the same as cry it out?

No. Ferber himself has said that equating his approach with simply letting a baby cry is a misunderstanding, and that it isn't the right fit for every child who sleeps poorly.2 The check-ins are the point: they reassure your baby, and you, that you're close, while still leaving room for your baby to learn to fall asleep on their own.

What the research says

  • They generally work. A review of 52 studies found most reported improvements in sleep.1
  • No clear winner. The same review found no method clearly better than the others.1
  • No sign of harm in the studies so far. A 2016 trial found graduated check-ins and bedtime fading improved sleep without signs of increased stress or attachment problems.3 A five-year follow-up of another trial found no long-term harm, and no lasting difference in sleep either.4
  • Studies are small. Most have dozens to a few hundred families, so there's still plenty that isn't known.

How to choose

  • Which can you stick to at 2am? This matters more than anything else.
  • How does your baby react to you? If your presence calms them, the chair method may suit. If seeing you leave makes it worse, fewer check-ins (or none) may work better.
  • Can both parents do it the same way? Pick the one you'll both follow.
  • Time: gentler methods usually take longer. That's fine if you're ready for it.

Switching methods

It's common and it's fine. In one survey of 652 parents, about a quarter ended up using a different method from the one they started with, most often moving from check-ins to no check-ins.5 If you switch, give the new method a full week before judging it.
