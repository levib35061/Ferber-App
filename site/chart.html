  • Wait only while they cry. If the crying stops, the wait stops. If it starts again, start the same wait over.
  • Check-ins are short: 1 to 2 minutes, a pat and calm words, no picking up. You're there to reassure, not to stop the crying.
  • After the 3rd wait, keep repeating that same number until they're asleep.
  • Night wakings: start again from the 1st wait for that night.
  • Nights 2 to 4 are often harder than night 1. That's normal. Most families see a real change within a week.

How to read the chart

Each row is one night. The three numbers are how many minutes to wait before each check-in. On night 1 you wait 3 minutes before the first check-in, 5 before the second, and 10 before the third and every one after that. On night 2 it's 5, 10 and 12. Each night the waits get a little longer.

Use the two empty columns to write down total crying time and the number of check-ins. Seeing those numbers shrink is what keeps most parents going on a hard night.

Common questions

Is this the official Ferber chart?

It's the widely shared version of the progressive-waiting schedule from Dr. Richard Ferber's book, Solve Your Child's Sleep Problems. Ferber describes the numbers as an example, not a rule. Check the current edition if you want the exact wording.

Can I use shorter waits?

Yes. Choose "Gentler" above for a slower build-up. What matters is that the waits get longer gradually and you stay consistent.

What happens after night 7?

Keep using the night 7 row. If there's no real change after two weeks, talk to your pediatrician about what to adjust.
