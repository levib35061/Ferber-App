# Night wakings and night feeds during the Ferber method

By the parent who makes Check In · Updated November 17, 2026

Bedtime gets most of the attention, but for many families the real test comes at 2am. Here's how to handle night wakings and night feeds so the progress you make at bedtime carries through the night.

**The short version.** Treat a night waking like bedtime. Start again from the **first wait for that night**, not where you left off. Ferber's words: "restart the schedule with the minimum waiting time for that night."[1](#s1) If your pediatrician says your baby still needs a night feed, give it at a planned time, then put your baby back down awake. Most other feeds can be reduced gradually over several nights.

## Which wait to use at a night waking

Each waking is a fresh start at the first wait for *tonight's* row of the chart. The night number doesn't change at 2am: a waking on night 3 starts at night 3's first wait.

First wait at a night waking, by night

| Night | 1st wait | 2nd wait | 3rd and later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 min | 5 min | 10 min |
| 2 | 5 min | 10 min | 12 min |
| 3 | 10 min | 12 min | 15 min |
| 4 | 12 min | 15 min | 17 min |
| 5 | 15 min | 17 min | 20 min |
| 6 | 17 min | 20 min | 25 min |
| 7 and after | 20 min | 25 min | 30 min |

So on night 2, a waking at 2am means: wait 5 minutes, check in, then 10, then 12 minutes each time after that until your baby is asleep again.

## Before you start the timer

Babies stir, grumble and even cry out briefly between sleep cycles, and many settle on their own within a minute or two. Starting the clock at the first sound, or going in to check, can wake them fully.

**Try:** give it a moment. Start counting once it's clearly sustained crying. If the crying stops before the wait is up, there's no check-in: the wait starts over if crying begins again.

## Night feeds: keep, plan, or wean

This is the question to settle **before** the first night, with your pediatrician. It depends on your baby's age, growth and feeding, and it's a medical question, not a sleep-training one.

- **If your baby still needs a feed:** keep it, but make it planned rather than a response to every waking. For example, the first waking after a set time. Feed calmly in low light, then put your baby back down awake. If they cry, the waits start again from the first one.
- **If your baby doesn't need it:** many families wean the feed gradually over several nights rather than stopping all at once (see below).
- **If you're not sure:** ask. "Does my baby still need to eat at night?" is a normal question at a check-up.

A feed that ends with your baby asleep at the breast or bottle tends to keep the wakings going, because that's how they learn to fall back asleep. Feeding is fine; falling asleep while feeding is the part to change.

## Weaning a night feed gradually

Ferber recommends reducing night feeds step by step, giving a little less and spacing feeds further apart, rather than cutting them off overnight.[2](#s2) In practice, families usually do one of these:

- **Bottle:** offer a slightly smaller amount each night.
- **Breast:** shorten the feed by a minute or two each night.
- **Either:** stretch the time before the first feed a little later each night.

Agree the plan with your pediatrician, especially for younger babies or if there are any concerns about weight. Wakings that are no longer fed are handled with the normal waits.

## Early-morning wakings

Pick a reasonable wake-up time, such as 6am. Wakings before it are night wakings: use the waits. At or after it, start the day properly with lights on, curtains open and a cheerful good morning, so your baby learns the difference. Ferber also advises starting each day at the same time, however the night went.[1](#s1)

**Try:** blackout curtains. Early light is one of the most common causes of 5am wakings.

## Who goes in at 2am

Decide in daylight. Many families take turns by night, so one parent gets a full night's sleep. Some find it helps if the parent who doesn't usually feed does the check-ins, so the visit isn't a reminder of milk.

Whoever is on duty does exactly the same thing: same waits, same short check-ins. Two different approaches at 2am is one of the most common reasons progress stalls.

## When to just go in

The waits are for protest crying from a healthy baby. Go to your baby, and comfort them, if they seem unwell, in pain, have a fever, have vomited, or sound different from their usual cry. Call your pediatrician if you're worried. You can pick the plan back up when they're better.
