# Restarting the Ferber method after illness, travel or teething

By [the parent who makes Check In](https://ferbertimer.com/about.html) · Updated November 19, 2026

Your baby was finally sleeping, then came a cold, a trip to see family, or a new tooth. Now bedtime is a battle again. This is very common, and getting back on track is usually much faster than the first time.

**The short version.** Pause sleep training while your baby is sick, and comfort them however they need. Once they're well, go back to the plan. After a break of a few days you can usually pick up where you left off; after a longer break, start again at night 1's waits. Most babies relearn within a few nights because they've already learned the skill once.

## When your baby is sick

A sick baby needs comfort, not a timer. Go in, feed, hold, and give medicine as your pediatrician advises. Call your pediatrician with any worry, and right away for a fever in a baby under 3 months.[2](#s-fever)

It's fine if old habits come back while they're ill: rocking to sleep, an extra feed, a night in your room. You aren't undoing all your work. Ferber's approach is about consistent routines once your child is well; a few unusual nights while sick don't change that.[1](#s-ferber)

## Restarting: pick up where you left off, or start over?

| How long the break was | Where to restart |
| --- | --- |
| A night or two | Where you left off. Use the next night's waits as if nothing happened. |
| About a week | Go back a night or two on the chart, or start again at night 1 if crying is back to the first nights' levels. |
| Several weeks | Start again at night 1. It usually moves faster this time. |

Either choice is fine. What matters most is picking one plan and sticking to it for the week.

**Try:** wait until your baby has been well for a day or two, with no fever and eating normally, before you restart.

## Travel and staying with family

New rooms, new cribs and new noises unsettle most babies. A few things keep the damage small:

- **Bring the familiar parts:** the sleep sack, white noise, the same bedtime routine in the same order.
- **Room sharing in a hotel** is the same as at home in your room: block the view of your bed if you can, and keep check-ins short.
- **Relax the rules** if you need to. A few nights of extra help on holiday is normal. Restart the plan the first night you're home.
- **Time zones:** move to local time from the first day for short trips across one or two hours; light in the morning and dim evenings help.

## Teething and "regressions"

Teething is often blamed for weeks of bad sleep, but sore gums often affect only a few nights around a tooth coming through. If your baby is clearly in pain, comfort them and ask your pediatrician about pain relief. If they settle as soon as you pick them up and seem fine in the day, it's more likely habit than teeth.

So-called sleep regressions often line up with new skills: rolling, sitting, crawling, pulling to stand. A baby who pulls up in the crib and can't get back down will cry for help. Practise getting down from standing in the day, and at night lay them down once at a check-in, then leave as usual.

## Daylight saving time

When the clocks change, either switch bedtime straight to the new time, or shift it by 15 minutes a day over a few days. Both work. Keep morning wake-up on the new time from the first day; it sets the rest of the day.
