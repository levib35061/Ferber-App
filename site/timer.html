Free Ferber method timer

Pick the night you're on. When your baby starts crying, start the wait. The timer counts down to each check-in, and the waits get longer each time, following the standard Ferber chart.

Keep this page open. A web page can't alert you once your phone locks or you switch apps. The timer keeps the right time if you come back, and it tries to keep your screen on while a wait is running, but it can't wake you. Dim your screen instead of locking it.

How to use it

  1. Put your baby down drowsy but awake, say goodnight, and leave.
  2. If they cry, tap "Crying: start the wait". If they settle without crying, you don't need the timer at all.
  3. When the timer says it's time, go in for a minute or two: a pat and a few calm words, without picking them up. You're there to reassure, not to stop the crying.
  4. Tap "Back out" as you leave. The next, longer wait starts.
  5. If the crying stops, tap "Stopped crying". The same wait starts over if it begins again.
  6. If they wake later in the night, tap "Woke up". The waits start again from the first one.

Common questions

Is this timer really free?

Yes. It runs in your browser with nothing to download and no sign-up. It doesn't track you; your place in the night is saved only on your own device, so a refresh doesn't lose it.

How long do I wait on each night?

On the standard chart: night 1 is 3, then 5, then 10 minutes. Night 2 is 5, 10, then 12. Night 3 is 10, 12, then 15. By night 7 it's 20, 25, then 30. After the third wait, keep repeating the last number. The full chart is here.

Can I use shorter waits?

Yes. Ferber himself describes the chart as an example. The "Gentler" option builds up more slowly. What matters is that waits get longer gradually and that you stay consistent.

What if I want to skip a check-in?

Tap "Wait a little longer" when a check-in is due. The next, longer wait starts. Some babies get more upset by check-ins, and fewer can work better.
